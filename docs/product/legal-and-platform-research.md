# Destra launches safely by keeping doctors in charge

Destra can be built legally in Brazil, but only if the product is designed so that **the doctor stays the approving publisher of every post**, so that reference-account analysis runs **only through official APIs, and only where they exist**, and so that data protection paperwork (LIA, RIPD, DPO, ANPD standard contractual clauses) is in place before the first tenant connects. Four findings drive the design. First, **CFM Res. 2.454/2026 says AI use does not exempt the doctor from the Code of Ethics, and AI used for advertising must "rigorously" follow medical advertising rules**. Liability for content therefore lands on the doctor, and Destra's own exposure shrinks only if the doctor actually reviews what goes out. Second, the only platform-sanctioned way to read other creators' content at useful depth is **Instagram Business Discovery, and that requires the Facebook Login flow with each doctor's Professional IG account linked to a Facebook Page**. YouTube's public Data API and X's paid reads are the other routes. **TikTok and LinkedIn offer no legitimate commercial API for reading other creators' posts**, and LinkedIn has even closed `r_member_social`, so Destra cannot read back a doctor's own historical LinkedIn posts. Third, LGPD treats public profiles as personal data. That makes Destra a controller for any cross-tenant reference corpus, and **since 23 Aug 2025 the ANPD's standard contractual clauses must be signed verbatim** with every US LLM or cloud vendor. Fourth, the AI bill **PL 2338/2023 is still pending in the Chamber**, with its vote pushed past the October 2026 elections. Destra therefore faces no AI statute yet, but it should build labeling and risk-assessment hooks now. The recommended rollout is Instagram + Facebook Pages first, with a CFM/ANVISA rule engine and mandatory approval, followed by YouTube, X and Threads. LinkedIn should launch as publish-only, and TikTok as upload-to-inbox until its audit passes. Autopilot should come last and should be narrowly scoped.

## The doctor stays the responsible publisher, and the rules differ by council

### CFM 2.336/2023 loosened medical advertising but kept hard lines

Res. CFM 2.336/2023 replaced the restrictive 1.974/2011 regime and has applied since **March 2024**. Sources disagree between 11 and 14 March ([Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/publicidade-medica-nas-redes-sociais-resolucao-cfm-traz-atualizacoes/); [CRM-PI](https://crmpi.org.br/noticias/cfm-atualiza-resolucao-da-publicidade-medica-em-resolucao-no-2-333-23/)). It now permits selfies, videos and audio without sensationalism, **publishing consultation prices**, reposting patient compliments that contain no superlatives or promises of results, and, under conditions, before/after images ([Abrasso](https://abrasso.org.br/regras-publicidade-medica/publicidade-medica-cfm-2336-2023.pdf); [CFM news](https://portal.cfm.org.br/noticias/resolucao-libera-fotos-como-antes-e-depois/)). Three details matter for a rule engine. **Name, CRM/UF and specialty with RQE number must appear "na página inicial em todas as redes sociais"**, meaning the bio or profile header ([SBOT summary](https://defesa.sbot.org.br/assets/file/SBOT-PUBLICIDADE-MEDICA-Resolucao-CFM-2336-2023.pdf)). A doctor without an RQE may not present as a specialist, and when mentioning a postgraduate course must add "não especialista" (art. 13, which Abramepo is challenging in court; no outcome found) ([Jovem Pan](https://jovempan.com.br/brasil/medicos-contestam-na-justica-resolucao-do-cfm-que-exige-uso-da-expressao-nao-especialista/); [CFM note](https://portal.cfm.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/notacfm_18022025.pdf)). And **"publicações de pacientes repostadas serão consideradas como publicações próprias"**, so the checker must also run on reshared testimonials ([SBOT](https://defesa.sbot.org.br/assets/file/SBOT-PUBLICIDADE-MEDICA-Resolucao-CFM-2336-2023.pdf)).

Before/after is permitted only when shown with indications, satisfactory and unsatisfactory outcomes, and possible complications ([CRM-PI](https://crmpi.org.br/noticias/cfm-atualiza-resolucao-da-publicidade-medica-em-resolucao-no-2-333-23/)). One 2026 law-firm piece still claims it is "expressamente vedada". That conflicts with CFM's own news item and looks like a holdover from the old regime ([Trade Cavalcanti](https://www.tradecavalcanti.com.br/publicacoes/publicidade-medica-e-o-cfm-o-que-pode-o-que-e-vedado-e-o-que-vira-processo-etico)) **[UNCERTAIN]**. Given that ambiguity, the safe product default is to route any before/after image to mandatory human review with a structured checklist, rather than auto-approve it. Sanctions under Lei 3.268/1957 art. 22 escalate from confidential warning to cassation. **Irregular advertising is described as one of the main triggers of CRM sindicâncias**, and most cases start from complaints ([Trade Cavalcanti](https://www.tradecavalcanti.com.br/publicacoes/processo-etico-profissional-no-crm-fases-prazos-e-direitos-do-medico-indiciado); [Guaíra News, Oct 2026](https://www.guairanews.com/2026/10/28/postagens-redes-risco-registro-medico/)). That makes the visible violations the highest-yield checks: missing RQE, superlatives, guarantees and unconditioned before/after.

### Endocrinology, the launch vertical, sits on top of hormone and drug-advertising bans

Endocrinology is the riskiest possible first vertical for content generation, because hormone marketing is where CFM and ANVISA have been most active. **Res. CFM 2.333/2023 bans prescribing anabolic-androgenic steroids for aesthetics, muscle gain or sports performance**, while treating disease remains allowed ([CFM clarification](https://portal.cfm.org.br/noticias/cfm-divulga-nota-para-esclarecimento-sobre-a-proibicao-da-prescricao-de-esteroides-e-anabolizante)). ANVISA suspended compounded hormone implants ("chip da beleza") in **October 2024**, including their advertising ([Afya](https://portal.afya.com.br/saude/anvisa-proibe-implantes-hormonais-manipulados-e-emite-alerta-sobre-riscos)). It then partly re-regulated them via RE 4.353/2024, which keeps the ban on advertising to the public and requires a CID on prescriptions ([Femina/FEBRASGO](https://femina.org.br/article/implantes-hormonais-manipulados-novas-regras-da-anvisa/)) **[VERIFY number and date]**. On **1 Oct 2026, twelve medical entities asked ANVISA for a total ban** ([Sincofarma SP](https://sincofarmasp.com.br/2026/10/05/entidades-pedem-que-anvisa-proiba-implantes-hormonais-manipulados/)), so a stricter rule could arrive soon. Separately, Lei 9.294/1996 and RDC 96/2008 bar advertising prescription-only medicines to the public, explicitly including the internet and social networks, and ban imperatives such as "tome" or "use" ([ASCOFERJ](https://ascoferj.com.br/noticias/propaganda-de-medicamentos-na-internet-e-nas-redes-sociais/); [CRF-PR](https://www.crf-pr.org.br/noticia/view/9248/orientacao-ao-farmaceutico-dentro-e-fora-da-internet-propaganda-de-medicamentos-deve-seguir-a-legislacao)). ANVISA has opened a revision of RDC 96 aimed at influencers and social media, with no final text found ([Panorama Farmacêutico](https://panoramafarmaceutico.com.br/anvisa-revisara-regras-para-propaganda-de-medicamentos/)).

The endocrinology rule pack therefore needs blocking rules, not just warnings. It should block promotion of testosterone, oxandrolone, gestrinone or other anabolic steroids framed around "definição", "ganho de massa", "performance", "estética" or "anti-aging" without disease context. It should block any public promotion of compounded implants, and any promotional naming of Rx brands such as GLP-1 drugs. It should allow neutral education about drug classes. Res. CFM 1.999/2012 (anti-aging) and 2.314/2022 (telemedicine) were not sourced in this research and are described only from training knowledge. They point the same way: no "modulação hormonal" promotion, and no individualized dosing advice in captions or comment replies **[VERIFY both]**.

### CONAR and the consumer code add a disclosure layer

CONAR issued a **new Influencer Guide on 13 May 2026**, in force immediately. It distinguishes "publicidade por influenciador", "mensagem ativada" and "conteúdo orgânico", and treats influencer advertising as promotion plus some form of compensation ([Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/456124/guia-conar-2026-o-que-todo-influenciador-precisa-saber-agora)). Annex G of the CONAR code covers doctors, dentists and health services, and Annex Q covers testimonials ([Senado copy of CONAR code](https://legis.senado.leg.br/sdleg-getter/documento/download/368d5816-92f1-4ba1-8746-f421c1c3f7b3)). Destra needs a "sponsored?" toggle that forces an ad label, or blocks the post outright where the council bans brand association. The CDC's misleading and abusive advertising provisions apply on top, but their text was not sourced in this research **[VERIFY CDC arts. 36–37, 67]**. TikTok's own commercial-disclosure policy took effect **24 Sep 2026** and treats promotion of your own business as content that must be disclosed. Doctors promoting their clinic are squarely covered ([TikTok policy](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/regulated-commercial-activities)).

### The later verticals need separate rule packs, not a global policy

Before/after alone shows why one global policy fails. CFM permits it conditionally. The dental council (CFO) permits diagnosis and conclusion images under Res. 196/2019, with a new advertising chapter under review ([Posicione-se](https://posicionesee.com/regras-marketing-odontologico/)). The **nutrition council's new code, Res. CFN 856/2026 (DOU 25 Apr 2026), bans before/after and result images even with patient consent, and bans associating the nutritionist with food or supplement brands**. It also adds AI rules ([CFN](https://www.cfn.org.br/index.php/nutricao-na-midia/novo-codigo-de-etica-dos-nutricionistas-proibe-divulgacao-de-fotos-antes-e-depois-de-pacientes/); [Itatiaia](https://www.itatiaia.com.br/saude/conselho-federal-de-nutricao-publica-novas-regras-sobre-uso-de-ia-e-redes-sociais/)). Its effective date is unclear: one source says 90 days after publication **[UNCERTAIN]**. Psychologists must show name, "psicólogo/a" and CRP number, may not predict results, and are advised against testimonials ([CRP-MT](https://crpmt.org.br/orientacao-tecnica/publicidade-e-divulgacao-profissional); [CFP Nota Técnica](https://site.cfp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/SEI_CFP-0612475-Nota-Tecnica.pdf)). Lawyers under OAB Provimento 205/2021 may produce content and run paid ads, but may not promise results, solicit clients, or mention fees or discounts to attract clients ([Projuris](https://www.projuris.com.br/blog/provimento-205-2021/)). A "new provimento" is mentioned in 2026 but was not confirmed ([Desmistificando](https://desmistificando.com.br/novo-provimento-publicidade-oab-como-se-preparar/)) **[VERIFY]**.

### CFM 2.454/2026 puts AI-generated posts on the doctor's account

**Res. CFM 2.454/2026 was approved on 11 Feb 2026 and published in the DOU on 27 Feb 2026**, with a correction on 5 Mar and a 180-day adaptation period that ran to roughly August 2026. As quoted by secondary sources, its §1 says AI use "não exime o médico do cumprimento do Código de Ética Médica". Its §3 requires AI used "com finalidade mercantil, publicitária ou promocional" to "observar rigorosamente as normas éticas relativas à publicidade médica" ([APM](https://www.apm.org.br/uso-da-inteligencia-artificial-na-medicina-resolucao-cfm-2-454-2026/); [Conjur, Aug 2026](https://conjur.com.br/2026-ago-01/inteligencia-artificial-na-medicina-a-resolucao-cfm-2-454-2026-e-os-novos-contornos-da-responsabilidade-civil/)). It frames AI as "exclusivamente como ferramenta de apoio", with the physician as "responsável final", and gives patients a right to be told when AI is used in their care ([CFM portal](https://portal.cfm.org.br/noticias/cfm-normatiza-uso-da-ia-na-medicina); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2026/03/brazilian-cfm-issues-resolution-on-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-medicine)). Whether it requires an AI disclosure on _marketing_ posts was not established **[VERIFY against official PDF]**. Commentators disagree on whether developers can still be strictly liable for defective algorithms ([Conjur](https://conjur.com.br/2026-ago-01/inteligencia-artificial-na-medicina-a-resolucao-cfm-2-454-2026-e-os-novos-contornos-da-responsabilidade-civil/)). This is the single most decision-relevant norm for Destra. It makes an approval step plus an audit log (what was generated, which checks ran, who approved, when) the doctor's best defence before a CRM. That audit log is also Destra's best defence against a claim that its service was defective.

## LGPD treats every public profile as personal data, and Destra is a controller for its corpus

### "Public" does not mean "free to reuse"

Under LGPD art. 7 §§3–4 and §7, publicly accessible data stays personal data. It can be reused for new purposes only when those purposes are legitimate and specific, respect the context of publication, and preserve data-subject rights ([Lei 13.709/2018](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)). The ANPD said so again in its **August 2026 deepfake study: the LGPD applies even when data is publicly available** ([Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/anpd-publica-estudo-tecnico-sobre-deepfakes-e-protecao-de-dados-pessoais)). For reference profiles, the only workable basis is legitimate interest. That needs a documented balancing test (LIA) following the ANPD's February 2024 three-phase model ([Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/anpd-legitimo-interesse-lgpd/); [guide PDF](https://tce.pb.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/guia_legitimo_interesse.pdf)). **Legitimate interest is unavailable for sensitive data**, and that is exactly what comments on medical posts tend to contain.

The leading precedent is the **ANPD's July 2024 preventive suspension of Meta's AI training on Brazilian users' public posts**. The ANPD found legitimate interest inadequate because public images, text and video can reveal sensitive data, because users would not expect the use, and because opt-out was obstructed. It lifted the suspension in August 2024 only after a compliance plan with notices, an easy opt-out and exclusion of minors, and said there was no "carta branca" ([Aos Fatos](https://www.aosfatos.org/noticias/anpd-meta-dados-brasileiros-ia/); [Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/noticias/30/08/2024/meta-anpd-suspende/)). Destra does not train foundation models, but "analyze other people's posts to learn style" raises the same questions. Meta's mitigation plan is therefore Destra's template: restrict the corpus to Professional/business accounts, exclude minors and private individuals, never retain commenter identities, aggregate comment themes at ingestion and suppress health self-disclosures, publish a non-user notice, and offer an opt-out with a suppression list. Scraping and data aggregators have been on the ANPD's priority map since 2024. The **2026–2027 map schedules roughly 20 AI-related inspections for 2027** ([Martinelli](https://www.martinelli.adv.br/anpd-publica-mapa-de-temas-prioritarios-2026-2027-e-atualiza-a-agenda-regulatoria-2025-2026/); [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/23/01/2026/anpd-define-75-fiscalizacoes-para-bienio-2026-2027-confira-prioridades/)). Law 15.352/2026 also turned the ANPD into a full regulatory agency with financial autonomy in February 2026 ([Senado](https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/42835651)).

### Patient images are sensitive data and need the patient's specific consent

Health data is sensitive (art. 5 II). Under art. 11 it can be processed only with **specific, highlighted consent** or another listed basis, and art. 11 §1 extends the rule to processing that can _reveal_ health data ([Lei 13.709/2018](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)). For before/after uploads, the doctor is controller and Destra is processor. Destra becomes a controller, and loses that clean position, the moment it reuses those images for a shared corpus or model improvement, so that reuse must be contractually and technically prohibited. Destra should ship a patient consent template that covers publication, AI processing and international transfer. It should require the doctor to attest to consent and upload the record, and it should switch LLM vendors to no-training and minimal retention.

### International transfers now require the ANPD clauses verbatim

Every prompt sent to a US LLM API and every byte stored in a US cloud is an international transfer. **The 12-month grace period of Res. CD/ANPD 19/2024 ended on 23 Aug 2025**. Since then, contractual transfers are valid only under the ANPD's standard clauses (CCPs), incorporated **without alteration**, or under ANPD-approved specific clauses. EU SCCs are not accepted as equivalent ([Lefosse](https://lefosse.com/noticias/alerta/transferencia-internacional-de-dados-prazo-para-adocao-das-clausulas-padrao-da-anpd-se-encerra-em-23-de-agosto/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2025/08/end-of-grace-period-implementation-of-brazils-standard-contractual-clauses-in-international-transfers-of-personal-data); [Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/en/unico/dpas-contractual-clauses/)). Hosting in São Paulo reduces transfers but does not eliminate them, because inference and foreign support access still count. Res. 19/2024 also appears to impose website transparency duties about transfers **[UNCERTAIN on exact wording]**.

### Roles, DPO, incidents and sanctions

The role map is asymmetric. Destra is the **processor** for each doctor's own account data, insights and patient uploads, so a tenant DPA is needed under arts. 37–39. It is a **controller** for the cross-tenant reference corpus, comment-theme aggregation and product analytics. A tenant who selects a reference profile may be a joint controller. Large-scale processing plus AI plus possible health data almost certainly counts as "high risk" under Res. CD/ANPD 2/2022, which strips the small-business simplifications ([Felsberg](https://www.felsberg.com.br/?p=41676)). The exact "emerging technologies" criterion is **[UNCERTAIN]**. Destra should therefore **appoint an encarregado (DPO) under Res. 18/2024**, who can be external, and publish the contact ([DOU via TSE](https://sintse.tse.jus.br/documentos/2024/Jul/17/diario-oficial-da-uniao-secao-1/autoridade-nacional-de-protecao-de-dados-resolucao-no-18-de-16-de-julho-de-2024-aprova-o-regulamento)). Incidents with relevant risk must be reported to the ANPD and affected individuals within **3 business days** under Res. 15/2024 ([Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/anpd-aprova-o-regulamento-de-comunicacao-de-incidente-de-seguranca/)). Fines reach 2% of Brazilian revenue, capped at R$50M per infraction, and private-sector fines have so far been tiny: the first was R$14,400 total, against Telekall ([Turivius](https://turivius.com/portal/multas-da-lgpd/)). **The realistic threat to a data-driven SaaS is a preventive suspension or deletion order on its corpus, not a fine.** Marco Civil art. 15 adds a duty to keep application access logs for 6 months ([Lei 12.965/2014](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l12965.htm)).

### Copyright favors storing features, not media

Brazil has no text-and-data-mining exception. Lei 9.610 art. 29 IX requires authorization to include works in a database, while art. 8 leaves ideas, methods and style unprotected ([Lei 9.610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)). The design that follows is to process reference media transiently and persist only derived features: hooks, structure, tone, cadence and topic taxonomy. Destra should filter outputs for near-verbatim copying and refuse "write as Dr. X" impersonation of a named competitor.

### PL 2338 is not law, but its shape is predictable

The Senate approved PL 2338/2023 on 10 Dec 2024. It has sat in the Chamber's Special Committee since March 2025, "Aguardando Parecer do Relator". On **24 Aug 2026 the rapporteur said the vote would come after the October 2026 elections**, and five planned vote dates have slipped since November 2025 ([Câmara](https://www.camara.leg.br/proposicoesWeb/fichadetramitacao?idProposicao=2487262); [DIAP](https://www.diap.org.br/index.php/noticias/noticias/92249-pl-2338-23-votacao-do-projeto-sobre-inteligencia-artificial-esta-prevista-apenas-para-dezembro)). Under the Senate text, health AI that assists diagnosis is high risk. Generative AI owes a preliminary risk assessment and synthetic-content identifiers, and commercial training on protected works would require remuneration ([Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/10/12/2024/pl-que-regula-ia-no-brasil-e-aprovado-no-senado/); [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/422014/ia-e-direitos-autorais-o-que-diz-o-pl-2-338-23-aprovado-pelo-senado)). Marketing-post generation would not be high risk. **It becomes high risk the moment Destra drifts into triaging patient DMs or suggesting treatments.** That boundary deserves to be a written product principle. No general Brazilian rule outside elections currently forces AI labels on commercial posts. Platforms label anyway: Meta applies "AI info" based on C2PA, and TikTok requires labels for realistic AI content ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/08/10/tiktok-ai-generated-video-label/)). Embedding C2PA metadata and offering a default disclosure line ("Conteúdo elaborado com apoio de IA e revisado por Dr(a). X, CRM …") is cheap insurance.

## Scraping is the wrong foundation, and the terms of use must survive the consumer code

### Platform terms ban scraping, and US case law does not protect a Brazilian SaaS

All four major platforms ban unauthorized automated collection. Meta's current terms cover scraping "whether conducted while logged in or not". Its Platform Terms also bar processing Platform Data "without valid User consent in order to build or augment user profiles" and bar buying or selling it ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta/meta-terms-of-service/automated-data-collection-restrictions/); [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms)) **[VERIFY exact text]**. X has barred using its API or content "to fine-tune or train a foundation or frontier model" since June 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/05/x-changes-its-terms-to-bar-training-of-ai-models-using-its-content)). LinkedIn bans crawlers and extensions that scrape or automate ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). Its Developer AI Policy allows some scopes only as "input/prompt to AI functionality (no training use cases)" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/developer-ai-policy)).

US courts have sided with _logged-off_ scrapers. In Meta v. Bright Data (Jan 2024), Meta lost and dropped its appeal. In X v. Bright Data (May 2024), contract claims were held preempted by copyright ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/); [FKKS](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102j7s5/blockbuster-ruling-federal-court-holds-that-copyright-act-preempts-xs-web-scrap)). But hiQ v. LinkedIn ended in a **$500,000 consent judgment, an injunction and deletion of scraped data** for logged-in and fake-account scraping ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case)). None of these doctrines binds the ANPD, and no Brazilian scraping judgment was found ([Baptista Luz](https://www.baptista.com.br/comercializacao-de-dados-pessoais-aplicacao-da-primeira-multa-pela-anpd-e-abordagens-internacionais-de-web-scraping/?lang=en)). The ANPD's first fine did involve scraped data.

For Destra the decisive argument is commercial rather than doctrinal. **Destra depends on Meta, TikTok, X and LinkedIn developer apps for publishing**, its core feature. Scraping those same platforms in breach of their user terms puts those apps at risk of revocation. Scraping also undermines the "good faith" leg of the LGPD balancing test. Vendors push the risk back onto the customer: Apify's 2024 terms make the customer indemnify Apify and exclude Apify's liability for unlawful use ([Apify terms](https://docs.apify.com/legal/old/general-terms-and-conditions-2024)). The vendor sits outside the liability chain, and Destra sits at its centre as controller.

### Third-party data vendors are cheap, but they buy risk, not compliance

If counsel nonetheless approves a narrow vendor use, for example TikTok reference discovery, the market is inexpensive. Pay-per-result scraping APIs run **roughly $0.75–$10 per 1,000 records**. For a workload of about 50 reference profiles × 30 posts per week on IG and TikTok, the research team estimated **$5–$150 per month**. Creator-analytics APIs run $200–$1,400 per month, and enterprise listening suites cost $9k–$93k+ per year and are poor at raw post content ([SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-instagram-data-apis-2026); [Bright Data](https://brightdata.com/solutions/data-extraction/tiktok); [Modash](https://modash.io/influencer-marketing-api/pricing)). All prices come from snippets, many from competitor blogs **[VERIFY]**. Consent-based Phyllo (from $199/month) only covers creators who connect their own account, so it **cannot pull arbitrary reference creators** ([Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/creator-data-api-vs-public-social-apis-what-should-you-build-on-in-2026)). No vendor publishes an SLA, and Brazilian influencer platforms (Squid, Influency.me, Air) expose no public data API. The recommendation is to keep vendors out of the launch architecture. If they are ever used, run them logged-off only, behind a feature flag, under a DPA with ANPD CCPs, with a kill switch, in a pipeline separate from the official-API apps, and never marketed as "scraping".

### Liability allocation: approval is the load-bearing defence

Marco Civil art. 19 is a weak shield for Destra. It protects providers against _third-party_ content, and the STF's June 2025 reinterpretation (Temas 987/533, clarified in June 2026 as fault-based, not strict) concerns hosting platforms. It does not reach content an AI drafts and the provider itself publishes ([Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/responsabilizacao-provedores-internet/); [Conjur, Jun 2026](https://www.conjur.com.br/2026-jun-08/plataformas-digitais-entre-o-stf-e-o-decreto-quem-define-as-regras-do-jogo/)). Destra's exposure would come through contract, the Civil Code's fault provisions, or CDC strict liability for a defective service. When the doctor approves each post, the harm traces to the doctor's own editorial act. **Unreviewed auto-publishing weakens that defence considerably.**

Whether a doctor counts as a consumer depends on the STJ's _finalismo mitigado_. A professional buying a work tool is generally not a consumer unless vulnerable, but vulnerability is presumed for natural persons ([Dizer o Direito, REsp 2.001.086/MT](https://www.dizerodireito.com.br/2023/12/em-regra-o-cdc-nao-e-aplicado-para-os.html); [TJMG 2024](https://modeloinicial.com.br/jurisprudencia/11000680/indenizatoria-produto-vicio)). An individual doctor on a click-through contract can plausibly invoke the CDC. The terms must therefore survive it. Offer a **7-day refund to everyone**, which neutralizes CDC art. 49 ([Jurídico.ai](https://juridico.ai/direito-civil/direito-de-arrependimento-art-49-cdc/)). Make auto-renewal clear, with one-click cancellation. Cap liability while carving out fraud and willful misconduct. Use a Brazilian forum, and sell negotiated B2B terms to clinics with a CNPJ. The terms should also make the professional the author and responsible party, state that AI output is a draft and not medical advice, make human approval the default with Autopilot as a logged, revocable and granular opt-in, ban patient data in prompts without a legal basis, and include a content indemnity, a takedown procedure and an LGPD DPA.

### Trademark and domain: file early, because INPI is slow

"Destra" is a common Italian and Portuguese word ("right hand"), so conflicts are likely. Search busca.inpi.gov.br for "Destra/Dextra/Destro/Dextro" in classes 9, 35 and 42, plus 41 and 44, before committing to the brand. The current INPI fee is **R$880 per class with a pre-approved specification, or R$440 with the 50% discount** for natural persons and ME/EPP. That puts three classes at about R$1,320–2,640 in official fees ([INPI fee table](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/custos-e-pagamento/NovaTabeladeRetribuiesINPI_MARCAS_Final_20_dez_25.pdf); [Portaria INPI/PR 10/2025](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/legislacao/arquivos/documentos/2025pr-10.pdf)) **[VERIFY effective date]**. A TCU audit found **average decisions took 19 months unopposed and 34 months with opposition** ([TCU](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/imprensa/noticias/tcu-analisa-sistema-para-solicitar-registro-de-marca-de-produto-no-inpi)). destra.com.br costs about R$40 per year and requires a regular CPF/CNPJ ([Nuvemshop](https://www.nuvemshop.com.br/blog/registro-br)). Register it under the company's CNPJ, not a founder's CPF.

## Each platform allows a different slice of the product

### Meta: Facebook Login is mandatory for the reference-account feature

Instagram offers two configurations. **Instagram Login** (graph.instagram.com) needs no Facebook Page and covers profile, media, publishing, comments, messages and, since 2025, insights. **Facebook Login for Business** (graph.facebook.com) requires the IG Professional account to be linked to a Facebook Page the user administers. Only Facebook Login unlocks **Business Discovery and Hashtag Search** ([Meta overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview/); [reference gist](https://gist.github.com/jameschapman2c/65eff9f54a2d350b17a6ce5127b9fe42)). No source found these added to Instagram Login by November 2026 **[VERIFY]**. Personal accounts have no API access at all, because the Basic Display API is gone.

Business Discovery (`GET /{own-ig-user-id}?fields=business_discovery.username(target){...}`) returns the target's public profile fields and paginated media with captions, like and comment counts, timestamps and Reels `view_count`. **It works only when both the doctor and the target are Professional accounts.** Hidden counts are omitted and must be treated as unknown, not zero ([Meta reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/business_discovery)). Hashtag Search needs the "Instagram Public Content Access" feature, is capped at **30 unique hashtags per user per rolling 7 days**, and returns no author usernames ([Meta overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview)). The onboarding implication is significant: many Brazilian doctors run Creator accounts with no linked Page. Destra must walk them through switching to Professional and creating or linking a Page. Doctors who refuse should get an Instagram Login fallback with publishing and own insights but no reference features.

Publishing is a container flow: `POST /media`, poll `status_code` until FINISHED, then `POST /media_publish`. It supports images, carousels of 2–10 items, Reels (all single videos become Reels) and Stories. The cap is **100 API posts per rolling 24h**, though some Meta pages still say 50, media must sit at a public HTTPS URL, and **there is no native scheduling** ([Meta content publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). Insights moved to `views` as the primary metric after `impressions` and `plays` were deprecated in v22.0 (April 2025). Requests cover a maximum 90-day range, data lags up to 48h, and Story insights exist only during the story's 24h life ([Meta v22 changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/version22.0)). Destra must snapshot metrics itself. No webhook fires for new media, so new posts have to be detected by polling against Business Use Case limits. Third parties put those limits at 4,800 × the account's 24h impressions **[VERIFY]** ([Meta rate limiting](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/overview/rate-limiting/)). Long-lived Instagram tokens last 60 days and can be refreshed once they are at least 24h old ([Meta refresh_access_token](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/refresh_access_token)). Pin calls to v26.0, released 29 Jul 2026 ([Meta blog](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2026/07/29/introducing-graph-api-v26-and-marketing-api-v26/)).

Going live to other businesses needs **Advanced Access via App Review per permission plus Business Verification** of Destra's company. Meta removed the separate Tech Provider "Access Verification" step in 2026, but the Data Use Checkup, Data Handling Questionnaire and Data Protection Assessment remain ([Meta App Review](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/app-review); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/meta-removes-access-verification-tech-provider-app-onboarding-12667685)). Reviews are often cited at 1–3 weeks, with first rejections common ([Postproxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/facebook-graph-api-posting-guide/)), so budget **1–2+ months** from CNPJ to live. A deauthorization callback and a data-deletion callback are mandatory ([Meta data deletion callback](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/development/create-an-app/app-dashboard/data-deletion-callback)). On disconnect, Destra should also purge reference data fetched with that doctor's token.

Facebook Pages publish via `pages_manage_posts` and, unlike Instagram, support **native scheduling 10 minutes to 30 days ahead** ([Meta Pages API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/pages-api/posts)). Threads reuses the container pattern, allows **250 posts per profile per 24h**, and needs its own OAuth flow and App Review ([Meta Threads](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/threads/overview/)).

### X: no review gate, metered cost

X moved new developers to **pay-per-use credits around February 2026**. Indicative rates are about **$0.015 per post created ($0.20 with a URL), $0.005 per post read, $0.010 per profile read, and $0.001 per "owned read" since 20 Apr 2026**, with a monthly cap of 3M post reads per app ([X pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing); [X Dev Community](https://devcommunity.x.com/t/x-api-pricing-update-owned-reads-now-0-001-other-changes-effective-april-20-2026/263025); [Outstand](https://www.outstand.so/blog/x-api-pricing)). Legacy Basic and Pro migrations reportedly happened on 1 Jun and 1 Sep 2026 **[VERIFY]**. Authentication uses OAuth 2.0 PKCE with `tweet.read tweet.write users.read media.write offline.access` and 2-hour access tokens. Media goes through the v2 upload endpoints, because v1.1 was retired on 9 Jun 2025 ([docs.x.com](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/authentication/oauth-2-0/authorization-code)). There is no native scheduling. Private metrics exist only for the author's own posts and only for about 30 days, so they must be snapshotted daily. Reference accounts expose only public metrics. A typical doctor costs well under $1 per month for 30 text posts. **Links in posts and reference reading drive the bill**: 10 references × 100 posts comes to about $5 per user per month, so Destra should meter it per plan.

### LinkedIn: publish yes, read back no

Sign In with LinkedIn (OpenID Connect) plus "Share on LinkedIn" (`w_member_social`) lets Destra post text, images, video, documents/PDF carousels and polls to a member profile with light review ([Posts API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/posts-api?view=li-lms-2026-09)). Whether member polls work under self-serve access is unclear **[UNCERTAIN]**. However, **`r_member_social` is closed, and LinkedIn is "not accepting access requests"**. Destra therefore cannot list a doctor's historical posts to learn their voice ([Upload-Post citing CM FAQ](https://www.upload-post.com/linkedin-api/)). Member Post Statistics (`r_member_postAnalytics`, with saves, sends and link clicks added in 202604) and follower statistics exist, but only inside the **Community Management API**. That API requires a Development tier, then a discretionary Standard-tier upgrade with a screencast. It reportedly has to sit on a separate developer app ([Member Post Statistics](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/members/post-statistics?view=li-lms-2026-09); [Increasing Access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/increasing-access)). Open-source schedulers are still waiting on that approval ([Postiz #1680](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app/issues/1680)). **No official API reads other members' or companies' posts**: `r_organization_social` works only for pages where the user holds a role. Access tokens last 60 days, and non-partners must re-prompt members. Data Storage Requirements reportedly limit member social activity to 48h and profile data to 24h, which conflicts with long-term analytics history and needs a careful read **[VERIFY]** ([Restricted use cases](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/restricted-use-cases)). Taplio-style Chrome extensions are a grey area, and LinkedIn reportedly scans for 6,000+ extensions ([Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/cybersecurity/news/linkedin-secretly-scans-for-6000-chrome-extensions-collects-data-4a39aadc-9c97-4c75-b157-c21d2db46110)). For voice learning and references, have doctors paste text or upload their LinkedIn data export.

### TikTok: audit-gated publishing, no reference reads

TikTok OAuth v2 gives 24h access tokens and 365-day rotating refresh tokens ([TikTok token management](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/login-kit-manage-user-access-tokens)). `video.list` returns the doctor's own public videos with view, like, comment and share counts ([TikTok List Videos](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/tiktok-api-v1-video-list?from_seo_redirect=1)). Reach and video views require a Business Account and a separate Business API integration ([TikTok Business API](https://business-api.tiktok.com/portal/docs/get-post-data-of-a-tiktok-account/v1.3)). Publishing comes in two modes. **Direct Post is restricted until audit: "Unaudited API Clients can allow up to 5 users to post in a 24 hour window" and only in SELF_ONLY viewership on private accounts** ([TikTok Content Sharing Guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines/)). Upload mode sends a draft to the creator's inbox, and the creator finishes the post in the app. TikTok also mandates the UX. Destra must query `creator_info` and display the creator's options, offer a privacy picker populated from the API, show comment/duet/stitch toggles and a commercial-content disclosure, and show a consent declaration before the publish button ([TikTok Query Creator Info](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-reference-query-creator-info)). Posting caps per creator are reported at about 15 per day across all apps **[VERIFY]**. The audit typically takes **2–6+ weeks**. One 2026 developer log shows about 3 weeks with several rejections, and another team needed six weeks and a dozen submissions ([GitHub PR](https://github.com/Themis128/cu130-slim/pull/174); [postwire](https://postwire.io/platforms/tiktok/approval/)). For other creators, the **Research API is limited to non-profit academics and bans commercial use**, and the Commercial Content API covers ads only ([apidojo](https://apidojo.io/blog/tiktok-api/research-api/); [TikTok Commercial Content API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/commercial-content-api)). That leaves consent (the reference creator connects their account), manual URLs, or vendors. Brazil availability was not confirmed either way **[VERIFY]**.

### YouTube: the most open platform, with 2026 quota and storage rules

Since **1 Jun 2026, `videos.insert` and `search.list` each have their own quota bucket**. The reported default is 100 uploads and 100 searches per day per project, plus 10,000 units for everything else. The old 1,600-unit upload cost cited by many guides is obsolete ([YouTube revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/revision_history); [determine quota cost](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost?authuser=50)) **[VERIFY: one Google page still says only 10,000 units]**. The upload cap is shared by all tenants, so **a quota extension and its compliance audit are needed once a few dozen doctors post daily**, and Google publishes no turnaround time ([Quota and Compliance Audits](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits)). Shorts are ordinary uploads, classified by vertical or square aspect ratio and up to 3 minutes. **Native scheduling works via `privacyStatus=private` + `publishAt`.** A revision entry dated **2 Nov 2026 reportedly lifts the 2020 rule that locked uploads from unverified projects to private** ([revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/revision_history)) **[VERIFY: very recent, and many 2026 guides still describe the lock]**. Other channels' public videos and stats can be read with an API key at 1 unit per call. Developer Policies, however, limit storage of non-authorized data to "limited amounts… not longer than 30 calendar days". From June 2026 they also reportedly reserve creating additional metrics or storing statistics for audited developers with approved analytics use cases ([Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)) **[VERIFY wording]**. Benchmark features built on other channels must be disclosed in the audit. Google OAuth verification for the sensitive `youtube.upload` scope is likely a separate process **[VERIFY]**.

## Build order and what must be done before launch

### Platform rollout recommendation

The rollout should follow where the legal and API ground is firmest. **Phase 0, starting now:** incorporate the CNPJ and start Meta Business Verification, which sits on everyone's critical path. Run the INPI search and file, register destra.com.br, appoint the DPO, and begin the LIA and RIPD. **Phase 1, the MVP:** connect Instagram via Facebook Login, with an Instagram Login fallback for doctors without a Page, and add Facebook Pages publishing. Ship the CFM 2.336 + 2.333 + ANVISA rule engine for endocrinology, mandatory per-post approval, the audit log, Destra's own scheduler, voice learning from the doctor's own posts, and reference analysis only through Business Discovery, cached and stored as derived features. Submit Meta App Review only for features already built. **Phase 2:** add YouTube Shorts with native scheduling (start the quota audit immediately), X (no review, cheap, metered reference reads), and Threads. Add LinkedIn publish-only with paste-in voice samples, and file for Community Management in parallel. Launch TikTok in Upload-to-inbox mode while the Direct Post audit runs, then switch to Direct Post with the mandated UX. **Phase 3:** add Autopilot, LinkedIn analytics once approved, TikTok Business API analytics, and new verticals with their own rule packs (CFO, then CFN, CFP and OAB), each signed off by counsel. Third-party scraping vendors stay out unless counsel approves a narrowly scoped, separated pipeline.

Autopilot deserves a firm constraint. Given CFM 2.454/2026 and the liability analysis above, it should mean **"publish on schedule from a queue the doctor has approved"**, not "generate and publish unseen". At most, it could cover low-risk template categories after an explicit, granular, logged opt-in, with the rule engine as a hard gate. Before/after images, drug mentions, prices, testimonials and anything the checker flags should always require a human.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Destra's core feature. "Analyze reference profiles" sounds like a data problem, but it is really a permissions problem. Across the five networks, the official APIs let Destra read others' content at depth on only one (Instagram, and only through the Page-linked Facebook Login path). YouTube allows reads under 30-day storage limits, and X allows them for a fee. On TikTok and LinkedIn the honest product is manual input or the reference creator's own consent. Building the corpus on what platforms sanction keeps the publishing apps safe and strengthens the LGPD balancing test. The better competitive edge is likely the compliance layer itself rather than a broader corpus: a per-council rule engine that knows RQE requirements, hormone and Rx bans, and the divergent before/after rules is something general-purpose schedulers lack, and something CRM complaints make valuable.

The second insight concerns liability. CFM 2.454/2026 places responsibility on the doctor, but that protection holds only as long as the doctor actually exercises editorial control. Every step toward unreviewed Autopilot moves legal exposure from the doctor's ethics file toward Destra's service-defect liability. That argues for Autopilot as a scheduling convenience over approved content, not as autonomous publishing, at least until PL 2338's final Chamber text and the first CRM decisions on AI-generated advertising show where the line is drawn.
